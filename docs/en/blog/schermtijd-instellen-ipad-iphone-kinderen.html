Setting Up Screen Time on iPad and iPhone for Kids: A Practical Guide

Maarten

Co-founder

Does this sound familiar? You hand your child the iPad for a quick episode of a favorite show or a relaxing Minecraft session, and before you know it, hours have flown by. As a parent, you want your child to experience the creative possibilities of technology — without falling into late-night scrolling, surprise in-app purchases, or daily arguments.

Apple provides powerful built-in Screen Time tools on iOS and iPadOS. But how do you configure them effectively? And more importantly: what can settings achieve, and what do they miss?

"Settings are the safety net, habits are the foundation."

The 4 Essential Apple Settings for Your Family

When configuring an iPad or iPhone for a child aged 4 to 12, these four controls deliver the most immediate peace of mind:

1. Downtime

Set a scheduled evening and overnight window where non-essential apps are automatically locked (for example, between 7:30 PM and 7:00 AM).
Path: Settings → Screen Time → Downtime. Be sure to enable 'Block at Downtime' with a 4-digit Screen Time passcode your child does not know.

2. App Limits by Category

Prevent games or streaming platforms (like YouTube or Roblox) from consuming the whole day. You can set daily allowances per category or specific app (e.g. 1 hour daily for recreational games).
Path: Settings → Screen Time → App Limits.

3. In-App Purchase and Store Restrictions

Block accidental credit card charges for Robux, V-Bucks, or virtual game currencies.
Path: Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions → Store Purchases. Set In-app Purchases to 'Don't Allow' and require password entry for any app installation.

4. Communication Safety & Content Filters

Enable on-device detection of sensitive images in Messages and AirDrop, and restrict adult websites in Safari.
Path: Settings → Screen Time → Communication Safety.

Why Settings Alone Are Not Enough

No matter how sophisticated Apple’s parental controls become, kids are resourceful. They quickly trade bypass tricks on the school playground. More importantly, technical limits cannot teach a child why balance and rest matter.
